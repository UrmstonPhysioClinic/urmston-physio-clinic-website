Dry Needling and Acupuncture in Urmston

Roxanna and Diana use dry needling and acupuncture as part of physio treatment, when it's appropriate for you. There's no separate appointment: book a physio assessment or follow-up as normal, and your physio will talk it through with you.

The Basics

How It Works

Dry needling and acupuncture both use very fine, sterile, single-use needles. Depending on your problem, your physio may place them in tight, tender areas of muscle, or at points that can be away from where you feel the pain. Some are in for a few seconds, others for several minutes.

Your physio will choose the approach that suits your problem, if needling is right for you at all, and explain what they're doing and why.

Neither is a treatment on its own. We only use them as one part of your physiotherapy, alongside exercise and advice.

What It's Used For

When We Might Use It

Dry needling and acupuncture may help some people with certain types of muscle and joint pain, as part of a wider treatment plan. They don't work for everyone, and they are never the whole treatment.

If your physio thinks needling could help, they'll explain why and what's involved. It's always your choice, and we'll plan your treatment without it if you'd rather not.

What It Feels Like

Before, During and After

  • 01
    BeforeYour physio checks your medical history to make sure it's safe for you, explains what's involved and asks for your consent.
  • 02
    DuringThe needles are much finer than the ones used for injections or blood tests. Most people feel a small prick, then a dull ache, tingling or heaviness. Sometimes the muscle twitches briefly.
  • 03
    AfterwardsSome soreness, a little bleeding or a small bruise is common, and some people feel tired or light-headed for a short while. Serious side effects are rare.

Safety

Who It Isn't Suitable For

Tell your physio if any of these apply. It may mean needling isn't right for you, or that we need to take extra care.

Fear of needles

If needles make you very anxious or faint, we'll use other treatments.

Pregnancy

Tell us if you're pregnant or could be. Some points are avoided, and we may choose not to use needling.

Bleeding risk

If you take blood thinners or have a bleeding disorder.

Weakened immune system

From a medical condition or medicines, which raises the risk of infection.

Epilepsy or fainting

If you have epilepsy, or have fainted with needles or injections before.

Implanted devices

Such as a pacemaker, especially if electrical stimulation might be used.

Lymphoedema

If you have lymphoedema or have had lymph nodes removed near the area.

Skin problems

Broken skin, a rash or an infection in the area. We also need to know about any metal allergy.
