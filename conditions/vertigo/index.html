Vertigo Physio in Urmston

Vertigo and dizziness can make everyday life feel unsteady and worrying. Vestibular physiotherapy is treatment for problems with the balance system in your inner ear. Your physio will work out what's likely to be causing your symptoms and, where it's appropriate, treat it with gentle head positions and exercises.

About This Condition

Understanding Vertigo

Vertigo is the feeling that you or the room is spinning. Dizziness is a broader word that can also mean feeling light-headed, off-balance or swimmy.

One of the most common causes is BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo). Tiny crystals in the inner ear move into the wrong place and cause short bursts of spinning when you move your head, for example rolling over in bed, looking up or bending down.

Other problems with the inner ear's balance system can cause longer-lasting dizziness and unsteadiness. These can often improve with vestibular rehabilitation exercises.

Signs and Symptoms

Common Symptoms

Your physio will ask about your symptoms in detail, as the pattern tells us a lot about the likely cause.

Spinning With Head Movement

Short bursts of spinning, often less than a minute, when you roll over, look up or bend down.

Unsteadiness

Feeling off-balance when walking, especially in the dark or on uneven ground.

Busy Places

Supermarkets, crowds or scrolling on a screen making you feel dizzy or unwell.

Nausea

Feeling sick during or after a dizzy spell.

Avoiding Movement

Keeping your head still or avoiding certain positions because you're worried about setting it off.

Our Approach

How We Assess and Treat Vertigo

  • 01
    Detailed historyWhen it started, what sets it off, how long the spells last and any other symptoms. This tells us a lot about the likely cause, and whether you should see a doctor instead.
  • 02
    AssessmentChecks of your eye movements, balance and walking, and positional tests such as the Dix-Hallpike, which can show whether BPPV is the cause. These can briefly bring on your dizziness, and we'll talk you through it.
  • 03
    Repositioning manoeuvresIf you have BPPV, a series of head and body positions, such as the Epley manoeuvre, aims to move the crystals back to where they belong. Many people with BPPV improve after one or a few treatments.
  • 04
    Vestibular rehabilitationExercises for your eyes, head and balance that help your brain adapt, for dizziness that isn't BPPV or that lingers afterwards. They're done little and often at home.
  • 05
    Getting back to normalGradually returning to the movements and places you've been avoiding, with advice on staying safe and lowering your risk of falls.

When to call 999 or see your GP instead

  • Call 999 if dizziness comes on suddenly with any of these: face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred speech, a sudden severe headache, or new double vision. These can be signs of a stroke.
  • See your GP if you have hearing loss, ringing in your ears, or dizziness that keeps going or keeps coming back. If you suddenly lose hearing in one ear, contact your GP or NHS 111 the same day.

Your First Visit

What to Expect

  1. We take a detailed history of your symptoms.
  2. We check your eyes, balance and walking, and do positional tests if they're suitable.
  3. If it's BPPV, treatment often starts in the same session.
  4. You leave with advice and, where it helps, exercises to do at home.

Some tests can make you dizzy for a short while, so you may prefer someone else to drive you home.
