Who It's For

Physio for Runners

We see runners of all levels, from beginners working towards their first 5k to club runners and marathon runners. Most running injuries build up gradually, when training increases faster than your body can adapt, rather than from one bad step.

That's why rest alone often doesn't fix them for good. Physiotherapy for running injuries looks at what changed in your training, how you move, and how strong the injured area is, then builds you back up step by step.

Common Problems

Running Injuries We Often See

Every runner is different, and your physio will assess your symptoms properly. These are the problems we see most often.

Runner's Knee

Pain around or behind the kneecap, often worse on stairs, hills or after sitting for a while.

ITB Pain

Pain on the outside of the knee that often starts at the same point in every run.

Shin Splints

Aching along the inside edge of the shin, often after a jump in mileage.

Achilles Tendinopathy

Stiffness and pain in the Achilles, usually worst first thing in the morning or at the start of a run.

Plantar Fasciitis

Pain under the heel, especially with your first steps of the day.

Calf Strains

A sudden pull or tightness in the calf, sometimes with a sharp pain mid-run.

Hamstring Injuries

Pain at the back of the thigh, from a sudden strain to a slow-building ache high up near the buttock.

Our Approach

How Your Running Injury Physio Helps

Every plan is built around you and your running. A typical course looks like this.

  • 01
    Find the causeWe go through your training, any recent changes and how the pain behaves, then look at how you move and test the strength around the injured area.
  • 02
    Manage the loadRather than stopping completely, we usually adjust your running so the injury can settle: shorter runs, an easier pace, fewer hills or more rest days, depending on what aggravates it.
  • 03
    Build strengthA progressive strength programme for the injured area and the rest of the leg, which you can do at home or in the gym. Exercise is a key part of treatment for most running injuries.
  • 04
    Hands-on treatmentWhere it helps, hands-on treatment to ease pain and stiffness while the strength work builds up.
  • 05
    Return to runningA staged plan back to your normal training, with clear signs to watch for, so you know when to push on and when to ease off.

Keep Running or Rest?

When to Keep Running and When to Stop

This is general guidance. Your physio will give you advice for your own injury.

Usually fine to keep running, with changes

  • The pain stays mild during the run
  • It doesn't get worse as the run goes on
  • It's back to its usual level by the next morning
  • You're not changing the way you run to protect it

Stop and get it checked

  • You're limping, or running differently because of the pain
  • The pain is sharp, or keeps building during the run
  • Pain at night or at rest, or one very tender spot on a bone, which can be a sign of a stress fracture
  • Swelling that doesn't settle

When to see your GP or call 999 instead

  • Call 999 for chest pain, severe breathlessness or collapse during or after running.
  • Get urgent advice the same day (NHS 111 or your GP) for calf pain with swelling, warmth or redness, especially after a long journey or an operation.
  • See your GP if your pain comes with a fever or you feel generally unwell.

Your First Visit

What to Expect

  1. We talk through your injury, your running and anything you're training for.
  2. We look at how you move and test the injured area. Wear shorts or something you can move in, and bring the shoes you usually run in.
  3. We explain what we think is going on and agree a plan with you.
