Condition

Sciatica

Sciatica can be one of the most debilitating types of pain — affecting not just the back, but radiating down the leg and disrupting sleep, work, and daily life. The good news is that the vast majority of people with sciatica recover well with the right physiotherapy treatment, and surgery is rarely necessary. Our physiotherapists are experienced in assessing and treating sciatic nerve pain, and will develop a treatment plan aimed at resolving your symptoms as quickly as possible.

About This Condition

Understanding Sciatica

Sciatica is not a diagnosis in itself — it describes pain along the sciatic nerve, which can be caused by several different things including a disc herniation, piriformis syndrome, spinal stenosis, or joint irritation. Identifying the specific cause is essential to providing effective treatment, which is why our thorough assessment is so important.

Once we understand what is compressing or irritating the nerve, we can use a combination of manual therapy, neural mobilisation, and progressive exercise to reduce the compression, restore nerve mobility, and address the underlying vulnerability that caused the problem.

Signs & Symptoms

Common Symptoms

These are the most common presentations we see — though every patient is different, and your physiotherapist will assess your individual symptoms thoroughly.

Radiating Leg Pain

Sharp, burning, or electric pain that travels from the lower back or buttock into the thigh, calf, and foot.

Numbness or Tingling

Pins and needles, numbness, or a "dead" feeling in the buttock, thigh, calf, or foot.

Lower Back Pain

Aching or sharp pain in the lower back, often accompanying the leg symptoms.

Weakness

Reduced strength in the leg or foot, particularly when walking, climbing stairs, or standing on tiptoe.

Pain on Sitting

Worsening symptoms when sitting, particularly on hard surfaces or in the car.

Variable Symptoms

Symptoms that change with position — often better when walking or lying down, worse when sitting or bending.

Our Approach

How We Treat Sciatica

Your treatment plan will be tailored specifically to you — but here is what a typical course of physiotherapy for sciatica looks like at Urmston Physio Clinic.

  • 01
    Nerve AssessmentA detailed examination of the sciatic nerve and lumbar spine to identify the specific cause and level of nerve involvement.
  • 02
    Neural MobilisationSpecific techniques to improve the mobility of the sciatic nerve and reduce neural sensitisation.
  • 03
    Lumbar Manual TherapyJoint and soft tissue techniques to reduce compression on the nerve root and restore spinal movement.
  • 04
    Progressive ExerciseA carefully graded exercise programme to stabilise the lumbar spine, reduce nerve irritation, and build resilience.
  • 05
    Education and Load ManagementGuidance on positions and activities to avoid in the short term, and a progressive plan for returning to normal activities safely.
