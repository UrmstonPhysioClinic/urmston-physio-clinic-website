Hip Pain

Hip pain can significantly affect walking, sitting, sleeping, and the ability to stay active. It is also a common source of referred pain into the groin, thigh, or even the knee — which means it is sometimes not immediately obvious that the hip is the source of the problem. Our physiotherapists are experienced in assessing and treating a wide range of hip conditions, identifying the true source of your pain and providing targeted, effective treatment.

About This Condition

Understanding Hip Pain

The hip is a deep joint with multiple potential sources of pain — including the joint itself, the surrounding muscles and tendons, the bursa, the labrum, and structures referred from the lumbar spine. Getting the diagnosis right is the most important first step, and it requires a skilled and thorough assessment.

Our treatment approach is tailored to the specific cause. For arthritic hips, we focus on maintaining mobility and reducing load. For tendinopathies, we use a progressive loading programme. For impingement or labral problems, we address the hip mechanics that are causing the impingement. Every plan is built around you.

Signs & Symptoms

Common Symptoms

These are the most common presentations we see — though every patient is different, and your physiotherapist will assess your individual symptoms thoroughly.

Groin or Front of Hip Pain

Deep aching or sharp pain in the front of the hip or groin, often associated with intra-articular hip problems.

Outer Hip Pain

Pain on the outside of the hip or buttock, often associated with gluteal tendinopathy or trochanteric bursitis.

Stiffness

Reduced range of motion when rotating the hip, getting in and out of a car, or crossing the legs.

Pain on Walking or Standing

Discomfort that worsens with prolonged walking or standing, sometimes improving with rest.

Clicking or Clunking

Audible or palpable snapping in the hip with movement, sometimes but not always painful.

Referred Knee Pain

Knee pain that actually originates from the hip — a common and frequently missed presentation.

Our Approach

How We Treat Hip Pain

Your treatment plan will be tailored specifically to you — but here is what a typical course of physiotherapy for hip pain looks like at Urmston Physio Clinic.

  • 01
    Hip and Lumbar AssessmentA thorough evaluation of the hip joint, lumbar spine, pelvis, and lower limb to identify the cause of your hip pain — including referred pain from the back.
  • 02
    Manual TherapyJoint mobilisation, soft tissue techniques, and hip traction to reduce pain and improve joint mobility.
  • 03
    Progressive Loading ProgrammeEvidence-based exercise for gluteal and hip tendinopathies, building tendon capacity progressively to restore pain-free function.
  • 04
    Hip Arthritis ManagementExercise and manual therapy programme specifically designed to manage hip osteoarthritis — reducing pain, improving mobility, and optimising function.
  • 05
    Movement and BiomechanicsIdentifying and addressing movement patterns that are placing excessive load on the hip joint, with targeted correction.

FAQs about Hip Pain

Physiotherapy can reduce pain, improve range of motion, strengthen the muscles around the hip, and help you stay active for longer. Many people manage hip arthritis very effectively with physiotherapy and avoid or significantly delay surgery.

Hip osteoarthritis involves degeneration of the cartilage within the hip joint itself. Hip bursitis (or greater trochanteric pain syndrome) involves inflammation of the bursa on the outer hip, caused by tendon irritation. They feel different and are treated differently — which is why an accurate assessment is so important.

Post-operative physiotherapy is essential after hip replacement surgery. A structured rehabilitation programme helps restore strength, mobility, gait, and function — converting a successful operation into a full functional recovery. We work with surgical team guidelines to progress rehabilitation at the appropriate pace.

Clicking in the hip is very common and often harmless, caused by a tendon snapping over a bony prominence. If the clicking is painful or associated with instability, it warrants assessment. Painless clicking alone is usually not a cause for concern but can be assessed if it is worrying you.
