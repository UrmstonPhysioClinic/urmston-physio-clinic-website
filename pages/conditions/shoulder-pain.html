Shoulder Pain

Shoulder pain can be remarkably restricting — affecting sleep, sport, work, and the simplest daily tasks like reaching up or putting on a coat. The shoulder is a complex joint with many potential sources of pain, which is why a thorough, specialist assessment is so important. Carl Healy, our Clinic Director, has a particular specialist interest in shoulder dysfunction and brings expert knowledge to the assessment and treatment of all shoulder conditions.

About This Condition

Understanding Shoulder Pain

The shoulder depends on a precise interplay between the rotator cuff, scapular muscles, glenohumeral joint, and cervical and thoracic spine. A problem anywhere in this chain can cause pain and restricted movement at the shoulder. Our assessment examines all of these components, along with your movement patterns and any contributing factors from the neck or upper back.

Carl Healy has a specific clinical interest in shoulder dysfunction and draws on advanced assessment skills to identify the precise source of your problem — whether that is a rotator cuff issue, impingement, joint instability, frozen shoulder, or something originating from the neck or thorax.

Signs & Symptoms

Common Symptoms

These are the most common presentations we see — though every patient is different, and your physiotherapist will assess your individual symptoms thoroughly.

Pain on Reaching or Lifting

Discomfort when raising the arm, reaching overhead, or lifting objects away from the body.

Night Pain

Pain that disturbs sleep, particularly when lying on the affected shoulder — a hallmark of many shoulder conditions.

Restricted Movement

Reduced ability to raise, rotate, or move the arm fully, sometimes with a painful arc of movement.

Weakness

Difficulty sustaining overhead activities or carrying loads due to weakness or pain-inhibited strength.

Clicking or Clunking

Audible or palpable clicking, snapping, or grinding in the shoulder with movement.

Stiffness

Progressive loss of range of motion in all directions, particularly characteristic of frozen shoulder.

Our Approach

How We Treat Shoulder Pain

Your treatment plan will be tailored specifically to you — but here is what a typical course of physiotherapy for shoulder pain looks like at Urmston Physio Clinic.

  • 01
    Specialist Shoulder AssessmentA comprehensive examination of the rotator cuff, glenohumeral joint, scapula, cervical spine, and upper thorax to identify the precise source of your shoulder pain.
  • 02
    Rotator Cuff RehabilitationTargeted strengthening of the rotator cuff and scapular stabilisers to restore shoulder stability and pain-free function.
  • 03
    Manual TherapyJoint mobilisation, soft tissue techniques, and neural mobilisation to reduce pain and restore shoulder movement.
  • 04
    Postural and Scapular RetrainingAddressing the thoracic spine posture and scapular control that underpins healthy shoulder function.
  • 05
    Return to Sport or ActivityProgressive rehabilitation to safely return you to overhead sport, lifting, swimming, or whatever activity is important to you.
