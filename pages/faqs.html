Getting Started

No — you do not need a GP referral to see us. Urmston Physio Clinic operates as a direct-access service, meaning you can book an appointment at any time without seeing your doctor first. This is one of the key advantages of private physiotherapy: you can get assessed and start treatment quickly, without waiting for a referral.

If you are using private health insurance, your insurer may require pre-authorisation before your appointment — please check your policy and obtain any required claim number before booking.

Yes, absolutely. As HCPC-registered physiotherapists, we are qualified to assess, diagnose, and treat musculoskeletal conditions independently. You do not need your doctor's approval or a referral letter — you can simply book online and attend your appointment.

That said, if you have a complex medical history or are recovering from a recent serious illness, it is always a good idea to let us know so we can factor this into your assessment and treatment plan.

Your initial consultation lasts 45 minutes. It begins with a detailed discussion about your symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, and goals. Your physiotherapist will then carry out a thorough physical assessment — observing your movement, testing your strength and flexibility, and examining the affected area.

Following the assessment, you will receive a clear explanation of what is going on, a diagnosis where possible, and hands-on treatment in the same session. You will also leave with a home exercise programme and a plan for your ongoing recovery.

Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing that allows access to the area being treated. For lower limb problems (hips, knees, ankles), shorts are ideal. For shoulder or upper back problems, a vest top or loose-fitting t-shirt works well. For lower back issues, comfortable trousers or leggings are appropriate.

If you are unsure, just bring a change of clothes or ask us when you book — we can advise you based on your specific condition.

The more information you can provide, the better. Useful things to mention include: where the pain or problem is located, when it started and how it began, what makes it better or worse, whether it has happened before, any treatment you have already tried, and how it is affecting your daily life, work, or exercise.

Do not worry if you cannot answer everything — your physiotherapist will guide you through the assessment with specific questions. There are no wrong answers, and everything you share is confidential.

Good questions to ask include: What is causing my pain or problem? How many sessions do you think I will need? What can I do at home to help my recovery? What should I avoid doing? How will I know if I am improving? When can I return to sport or normal activity?

We encourage all patients to ask questions at any point — understanding your condition and treatment is an important part of getting better. You will never be made to feel like your questions are unwelcome or taking up too much time.

Look for a physiotherapist who is registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) — this is the legal requirement to practise in the UK. Membership of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP) is also a strong indicator of professional standards. All physiotherapists at Urmston Physio Clinic meet both criteria.

Beyond registration, look for experience relevant to your condition, clear communication, and an approach that focuses on finding the cause rather than just managing symptoms. Patient reviews are also a useful guide.

Treatment & Recovery

This depends on your condition, how long you have had it, and how your body responds to treatment. Many patients notice improvement after their first or second session. For straightforward acute injuries, a course of 4–6 sessions is often sufficient. Chronic or complex conditions may take longer.

At your initial assessment, your physiotherapist will give you a realistic expectation of your recovery timeline based on your specific situation.

Frequency depends on your condition and stage of recovery. In the early stages of treatment, weekly sessions are common. As you progress, sessions may be spaced out to fortnightly or monthly to allow you to consolidate your progress between appointments.

Your physiotherapist will recommend the appropriate frequency for you and adjust this as your recovery progresses.

It is normal to feel some muscle soreness for 24–48 hours after your appointment, particularly if hands-on treatment or new exercises have been introduced. This is similar to the feeling after a good workout and typically settles quickly.

Most patients feel looser, less restricted, or have reduced pain immediately after treatment. If you experience significant pain that does not improve after 48 hours, contact us and we will advise you.

Signs that physiotherapy is working include: reduced pain levels, improved range of movement, greater strength or function, better sleep due to less discomfort, and being able to do things you could not do before. Progress is not always linear — you may have good days and less good days, particularly early in treatment.

Your physiotherapist will regularly reassess your progress and adjust your treatment plan accordingly. If progress is slower than expected, they will explore why and consider alternative approaches.

You are generally ready to stop physiotherapy when you have achieved your treatment goals — such as returning to full activity, being pain-free, or having the tools to self-manage effectively. Your physiotherapist will discuss discharge with you and ensure you have a clear home programme to maintain your progress.

We never keep patients attending appointments unnecessarily. Our aim is to get you better and independent as efficiently as possible.

Stopping too early is one of the most common reasons why problems recur. Pain often reduces before the underlying cause has been fully addressed — the tissues may still be healing, or the movement patterns that contributed to the problem may not yet have changed.

If you are considering stopping before your physiotherapist has recommended discharge, please discuss it with them. There may be ways to reduce the frequency of appointments while ensuring your recovery is consolidated.

The single biggest factor in recovery speed is consistency with your home exercise programme. Attending sessions is important, but what you do between appointments matters just as much. Other helpful factors include adequate sleep, good nutrition, staying active within your limits, and keeping stress levels manageable where possible.

Communicate openly with your physiotherapist about how things are going. If exercises are too difficult or causing more pain, let them know so the programme can be adjusted.

Yes — home exercises are a fundamental part of physiotherapy treatment. At every stage of your recovery, your physiotherapist will prescribe specific exercises tailored to your condition and abilities. These are designed to complement in-clinic treatment and are essential to achieving lasting results.

You do not need specialist equipment for most exercises. Your physiotherapist will ensure you understand how to perform each exercise correctly before you leave the clinic.

For your home exercise programme, you will need a small amount of space — enough to lie on a mat or stand with your arms extended. A yoga mat is useful but not essential. Some exercises may require a resistance band or light weights, but your physiotherapist will advise you on this and suggest affordable options if needed.

Most importantly, set aside a consistent time each day to do your exercises. Routine makes compliance much easier.

The primary tools of physiotherapy are the physiotherapist's hands — manual therapy, soft tissue techniques, joint mobilisation, and manipulation are all hands-on treatments. We also use exercise prescription, movement analysis, and patient education as core treatment approaches.

Depending on the condition, additional tools may include resistance bands, foam rollers, massage tools, and postural aids. We do not rely on passive treatments like ultrasound or TENS as primary interventions, preferring evidence-based active approaches that produce lasting results.

Massage focuses on the soft tissues — muscles and connective tissue — and is primarily aimed at relaxation, improved circulation, and short-term pain relief. It does not typically include assessment or diagnosis, and does not address the underlying causes of pain or movement dysfunction.

Physiotherapy is a clinical discipline that involves comprehensive assessment, diagnosis, and a range of evidence-based treatments including manual therapy (which may include massage-like techniques), exercise prescription, movement re-education, and patient education. The aim is to identify and resolve the root cause of your problem — not just provide temporary relief.

Physiotherapy is unique in combining thorough clinical assessment with active, hands-on treatment and patient education. Unlike pain medication, which masks symptoms, physiotherapy addresses the cause. Unlike surgery, it is non-invasive. Unlike generic exercise, it is precisely targeted to your specific condition and presentation.

The evidence base for physiotherapy in treating musculoskeletal conditions is strong. For the majority of common MSK problems, physiotherapy is recommended as a first-line treatment before considering more invasive options.

Yes — back pain is one of the conditions we treat most frequently, and physiotherapy has a strong evidence base for its management. Whether your back pain is acute (sudden onset) or chronic (long-standing), we can help. Our approach involves identifying the specific cause of your pain rather than applying a generic treatment, which produces far better results.

The vast majority of back pain — even severe back pain — responds well to physiotherapy. You do not have to live with it.

Physiotherapists treat a very wide range of conditions. At Urmston Physio Clinic, we specialise in musculoskeletal physiotherapy, which covers: back and neck pain, shoulder problems (rotator cuff, frozen shoulder, impingement), hip and knee conditions, ankle and foot problems, sports injuries, post-operative rehabilitation, sciatica and nerve pain, headaches, arthritis, and movement and performance issues.

If you are unsure whether we can help with your specific condition, please call us and we will advise you honestly.

Yes — and this is an area we take seriously. Chronic pain (pain lasting more than three months) often involves a combination of physical, lifestyle, and sometimes psychological factors. Our approach to chronic pain involves a thorough assessment to identify all contributing factors, followed by a structured treatment plan that addresses both the physical drivers and the broader context.

We will be honest with you about what physiotherapy can achieve, and our goal is to significantly reduce your pain, improve your function, and give you the tools to self-manage effectively.

Yes. Physiotherapy is one of the most effective treatments for both osteoarthritis and inflammatory arthritis. While it cannot reverse joint changes, it can significantly reduce pain, improve joint mobility, strengthen the surrounding muscles, and improve your ability to manage daily activities.

Exercise — prescribed specifically for your condition and abilities — is one of the most evidence-based interventions for arthritis. We will design a programme that works for you and helps you stay as active and independent as possible.

Many headaches — particularly tension headaches and cervicogenic headaches (those arising from the neck) — respond very well to physiotherapy. These types of headaches often originate from stiffness or dysfunction in the cervical spine, and targeted manual therapy combined with specific exercises can produce significant and lasting improvement.

If you have been struggling with frequent headaches, it is worth being assessed to understand whether they have a musculoskeletal cause that physiotherapy can address.

Nerve pain — such as sciatica or nerve-related arm pain — typically occurs when a nerve is being compressed or irritated by a nearby structure. Physiotherapy helps by identifying the source of the compression (often a disc, joint, or tight muscle), using manual therapy and specific movements to reduce the pressure, and prescribing exercises to improve the nerve's mobility and the stability of the surrounding structures.

Nerve pain can improve significantly with the right treatment. Early intervention generally leads to better outcomes.

Yes. Poor posture is often a contributing factor to neck pain, back pain, shoulder problems, and headaches. Physiotherapy can identify the specific postural habits and muscle imbalances that are causing or contributing to your problems, and prescribe targeted exercises and movement changes to address them.

Postural correction is rarely about simply "sitting up straight" — it involves understanding and changing how you move, strengthening the right muscles, and often making changes to your workspace or daily habits. We approach this practically and realistically.

Yes — improving mobility and function is a core goal of physiotherapy. Whether your mobility has been reduced by injury, surgery, a neurological condition, or simply the effects of ageing and deconditioning, physiotherapy can help. Treatment will be tailored to your specific situation, abilities, and goals.

Acute pain (recent onset, usually within the first few weeks) is typically managed with a focus on reducing inflammation, restoring early movement, and protecting the injured area appropriately. The goal is to prevent the problem from becoming chronic.

Chronic pain (lasting more than 12 weeks) often requires a broader approach that addresses not just the physical injury but also the movement habits, lifestyle factors, and sometimes the nervous system's sensitisation that occur with long-term pain. Treatment tends to be more graduated and focused on building capacity over time.

Surgery & Injury

For many conditions, yes — physiotherapy can achieve equivalent or better outcomes compared to surgery, without the associated risks and recovery time. Research has shown this to be the case for a number of common conditions including meniscal tears, rotator cuff problems, lumbar disc herniation, and knee osteoarthritis.

We will always give you an honest assessment of whether physiotherapy is likely to be sufficient, or whether surgical opinion should be sought. In some cases, physiotherapy before surgery (prehabilitation) significantly improves post-operative outcomes.

Post-operative physiotherapy is essential for achieving the best possible outcome from surgery. Whether you have had a hip or knee replacement, ACL reconstruction, shoulder repair, or spinal procedure, a structured rehabilitation programme is what converts a technically successful operation into a functional recovery.

We have extensive experience in post-surgical rehabilitation and work closely alongside the recovery timelines provided by surgical teams. We can begin rehabilitation from as early as the first week post-surgery in many cases.

Recovery times vary significantly depending on the type and severity of the injury. A mild muscle strain may resolve in 2–4 weeks with appropriate treatment. A moderate ligament sprain may take 6–12 weeks. More significant injuries such as ACL ruptures or stress fractures can take 6–12 months to return to full activity.

The key is starting appropriate treatment early and following a structured, progressive rehabilitation programme. Rushing back too quickly — or resting completely for too long — both lead to worse outcomes.

Yes. Whether you have been involved in a road traffic accident, a fall, a workplace incident, or any other accident, physiotherapy can help with the physical recovery. Whiplash, soft tissue injuries, fracture rehabilitation, and generalised musculoskeletal trauma all respond well to physiotherapy.

If your accident was the result of someone else's negligence and you are pursuing a personal injury claim, we can provide treatment and, where required, clinical reports to support your case. Please contact us to discuss your situation.

As soon as possible after injury or onset of pain. Early intervention is associated with faster recovery, lower risk of the problem becoming chronic, and reduced overall treatment costs. Many people wait weeks or months hoping the problem will resolve on its own — and while some do, many do not, and the longer you wait, the more entrenched the problem can become.

If you are in doubt about whether your problem warrants physiotherapy, please call us. We would rather advise you that you do not need to come in than have you wait unnecessarily.

Older Adults & Prevention

Absolutely. Physiotherapy is safe and highly effective for older adults, and it is particularly important in this age group for maintaining mobility, managing arthritis, improving balance and fall prevention, and supporting recovery after surgery. Roxanna Healy brings extensive experience working with older patients across both neurological and musculoskeletal presentations.

All treatment is adapted to your individual capacity and health status. We will never push you beyond what is appropriate for your situation.

Yes — injury prevention is one of the most valuable applications of physiotherapy. By identifying movement problems, muscle imbalances, and biomechanical weaknesses before they lead to injury, physiotherapy can significantly reduce your injury risk. This is particularly valuable for runners, cyclists, gym-goers, and anyone who trains regularly.

Our movement and biomechanics assessment service is specifically designed for those wanting to stay ahead of injury rather than waiting for something to go wrong.

Restricted range of motion is usually caused by a combination of joint stiffness, muscle tightness, pain inhibition, and movement habits. Physiotherapy addresses each of these: joint mobilisation and manipulation improve joint mobility directly; soft tissue techniques address muscle and fascial tightness; graduated exercise improves overall flexibility and strength; and movement re-education changes the patterns that may be limiting you.

This depends entirely on the cause of your knee pain — the right exercises for patellofemoral pain are very different from those for an ACL injury or knee arthritis. Generic knee exercises from the internet can sometimes help, but they can also aggravate the wrong structure if the cause has not been properly identified.
